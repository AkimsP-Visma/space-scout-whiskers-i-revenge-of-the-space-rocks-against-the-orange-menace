# Scout and the ringed planet - brief

## Spec
- 16:9, 1920x1080, 24fps, 28.0s (672 frames), 120 BPM (a bar = 2s, an 8th = 6 frames, a 16th = 3 frames).
- Style: pixel (shipped kit), every shot at 320x180 (6 px per pixel), PAL16 enforced, soft CRT (scanlines 0.16, vignette 0.35).
- Story format: a fixed-hero journey (F5) with the journey as one continuous shot: the scout holds its screen spot (hull at pixel 112,100; the pilot is the anchor) from 0 to 12.5s while one world flows past it.
- Music composed in code (chiptune). No voice. No assets. Formats: 16:9 only.

## The idea
A little scout cruises toward a ringed planet, threads the gap in its rings, and comes out into sunlight.
Device: the planet starts as one odd star ahead of the ship and ends as one star behind it.

## Hero and colour
- The scout: a white ship with an orange stripe and engine; the pilot, an orange cat in the glass canopy (close up: a flight suit with an orange "07" patch).
- Orange means the scout and nothing else. Pink and purple mean the planet (the odd star, the bands, the halo). Everything else is cool: ink, night, blue, slate, grey, ice. The sun is white and yellow.

## Transitions
Four hard cuts, each a new camera on the same world: 12.5 (scale-out to the wide), 14.5 (close-up), 16.0 (into the gap), 19.0 (the flash into sunlight). Everything else is continuous: things enter and leave the frame on their own.
The first cut of the piece used F5's accelerating cuts (15 cuts, a 16th rush); the user found objects popping in and vanishing at the cuts read as stitched-together scenes, so the journey and the ending became continuous shots.
